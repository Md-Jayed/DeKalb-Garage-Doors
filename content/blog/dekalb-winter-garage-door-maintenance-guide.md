Winter in Northern Illinois brings harsh freezing temperatures, heavy snow accumulation, and road salt spray. Sub-zero weather can contract metal components, stiffen lubricants, and freeze bottom rubber seals directly to driveway concrete.

Following a pre-winter maintenance checklist helps prevent middle-of-the-night emergency breakdowns during cold snaps in **DeKalb, Sycamore, Cortland, and Genoa**.

---

## 4 Winter Prep Steps for Your Garage Door

### 1. Apply Synthetic Low-Temp Silicone Lubricant
Standard grease can harden and turn tacky in cold weather, causing rollers and hinges to bind. Spray high-performance synthetic silicone or lithium lubricant along:
* Steel rollers and ball bearings
* Torsion spring coils (prevents rust bonding)
* Hinge pivot points and metal tracks

*Note: Never apply heavy motor oil orWD-40 as a primary lubricant; WD-40 is a solvent and will strip away protective coatings.*

### 2. Inspect & Clean Bottom Rubber Weather Seals
If water collects under your door threshold, freezing temperatures will cement the rubber seal to the driveway. When the automatic opener tries to pull the door open, it can rip the bottom rubber seal right off the aluminum retainer or snap lifting cables.
* Wipe down bottom seals with silicone spray to prevent ice adhesion.
* Sweep away snow and slush accumulation before closing the door for the night.

### 3. Check Insulation Values (R-Value)
An uninsulated metal garage door allows icy air to permeate attached living spaces or rooms located above the garage. Upgrading to an **R-12 or R-16 polyurethane insulated door** maintains garage temperatures up to 20°F warmer during peak winter.

### 4. Test Auto-Reverse Safety Sensors
Cold weather condensation or snow drifts can obstruct photo-eye lenses. Ensure the infrared sensors are clean, aligned, and illuminating solid status indicator LEDs.
