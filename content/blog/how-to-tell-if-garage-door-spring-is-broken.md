## 5 Clear Signs Your Garage Door Spring Is Broken

### 1. You Heard a Loud Bang in the Garage
Many homeowners describe the sound of a snapping torsion spring as a gunshot or a heavy metallic pop coming from the garage. Even if the garage door was closed at the time and nobody was operating it, metal fatigue can reach a critical tipping point at any hour.

### 2. The Door Only Opens 3 to 6 Inches
When you push the wall button or remote transmitter, the automatic opener motor hums loudly, but the door only moves a few inches before stopping. This happens because modern garage door openers feature built-in safety force limiters that automatically abort operation when they encounter resistance beyond the motor's pulling power.

### 3. A Visible Gap in the Winding Coil Above the Door
Look above your closed garage door at the central metal shaft (for torsion systems) or along the side tracks (for extension systems). If you see a **2-inch to 4-inch gap** splitting a steel coil into two pieces, the spring has severed.

### 4. Loose or Sagging Lifting Cables
When a torsion spring snaps, tension instantly vanishes from the cable drums. This causes the high-tensile aircraft steel cables along both sides of the door to unspool, dangle loosely, or pop off the cable pulley wheels.

### 5. The Garage Door Feels Exceptionally Heavy
An unassisted garage door weighs anywhere from 150 to over 350 pounds. When springs are intact, you can lift a door manually with one hand. If attempting to lift the door manually feels like lifting concrete blocks, the springs are no longer counterbalancing the door's physical weight.

## Why DIY Garage Door Spring Repair Is Extremely Dangerous

Garage door springs store massive mechanical kinetic energy. Standard residential springs operate under high pressure to lift heavy insulated steel or solid wood panels thousands of times throughout their lifespan.

Attempting to replace or wind torsion springs without professional winding bars, vice grips, and specialized mechanical training can lead to severe physical injuries including:

* **High-velocity impact injuries** from slipping winding bars.
* **Limb entrapment** between cable drums and torsion shafts.
* **Secondary damage** to opener rails, panels, and track alignment.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I run my garage door opener with a broken spring?
**No.** Running an electric opener with a snapped spring will burn out the internal gear drive or strip the motor assembly. Openers are designed to propel doors, not lift unassisted deadweight.

### Should I replace both springs if only one broke?
Yes! Both springs were installed at the exact same time and have completed the exact same number of cycles. When one snaps, the remaining spring is almost guaranteed to fail within weeks.
